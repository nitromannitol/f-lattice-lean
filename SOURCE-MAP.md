## First proof's inputs

For `IsBurningRank.allowed`, the implicit field and rank, rank carrier and order are typing inputs from Section 9. The rank hypothesis expands to the two cases in Section 9 at every physical site. The finite vertex set and its nonemptiness are the induced subgraph in the conclusion of that argument.

For `finiteComparison_of_allowed`, the two fields and the finite set are the comparison data. The allowed-set hypothesis is exactly the forward-use hypothesis in `prop:recurrence`. The integer bound quantifies the pointwise form, with one hypothesis requiring that bound on the incoming boundary. The competitor assumption is restricted to the set because the proof only uses its Laplacian there. `IsBurningRank.finiteComparison` supplies the allowed-set hypothesis from the rank, and the separate endpoint module supplies the rank from the explicit endpoint function.

## Ordered height rule inputs

The ambient carrier in `HeightTile` is generic; the physical specialization is `Site`. Decidable equality is used only for finite support operations. The two tile heights have values on their supports, not elsewhere. The contact input to `contactShift` is needed for the minimum and follows from the equality witness of `Fits`. The shared target and old gauge represent $T+c$. A single offset in `Fits` is measured relative to $T$; `contactShift_eq_of_agrees` subtracts the old gauge from it, so the actual shift is measured relative to $T+c$. The new-site equality, overlap inequality and attained equality occur in that order in `Fits`. There is no hypothesis of agreement at every overlap site.

`OrderedComparisons` expands to these three tests at each insertion on the current prefix's union. It does not establish them for any source-specific list. `correctionHeight` acts on an already placed list (with nonempty first copy and the required contacts); it does not choose either correction placement or its short/long list.
